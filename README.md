# Thyroid Pathology and Cytopathology for AI Researchers

An engineer-oriented, image-centered self-study guide. **Educational only; not a clinical diagnostic protocol.**

## Learning objectives
1. Distinguish thyroid follicular and papillary tissue architectures.
2. Identify nuclear morphology features used in thyroid cytopathology.
3. Explain the difference between cytologic Bethesda categories and final histologic diagnoses.
4. Recognize what FNA cannot establish (especially capsular/vascular invasion and definitive NIFTP diagnosis).
5. Connect morphological uncertainty to AI labels, model evaluation, and dataset shift.

## Suggested six-week sequence (2–3 hours/week)
| Week | Topic | Practice |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Normal thyroid anatomy and histology | Sketch a follicle; identify epithelium, lumen, colloid |
| 2 | Architecture and neoplasia | Compare follicular, papillary, solid patterns; define capsule and invasion |
| 3 | Cytology and nuclear morphology | Annotate nuclear size, chromatin, grooves, pseudoinclusions in educational examples |
| 4 | Bethesda categories and sampling | Map six Bethesda categories to diagnostic uncertainty; list FNA limitations |
| 5 | FA, FC, PTC, FVPTC, NIFTP | Build a comparison matrix of architecture, nuclei, invasion, molecular associations |
| 6 | AI translation | Audit labels, patient-level splits, staining differences, calibration, and explainability |

## Study method
For every micrograph: (1) note specimen type and stain, (2) describe visible architecture or cell grouping, (3) describe nuclear features, (4) separate observation from diagnosis, (5) list what the image cannot establish. Use licensed/authorized images only; do not upload patient-identifiable material.

## Important distinction
**Papillary architecture** means finger-like epithelial projections, usually with fibrovascular cores. **Papillary-type nuclear features** are changes in individual nuclei. Follicular architecture and papillary-type nuclei can coexist. True papillae alone do not universally establish malignancy under current classifications.
