# Module 3 — From morphology to diagnosis

## Distinguish these levels
- **Cytologic interpretation (FNA):** Bethesda I nondiagnostic, II benign, III atypia of undetermined significance, IV follicular neoplasm, V suspicious for malignancy, VI malignant.
- **Histologic diagnosis (resection):** e.g., follicular adenoma, follicular carcinoma, papillary thyroid carcinoma, NIFTP.
- **Biologic behavior:** recurrence, invasion, metastasis, and outcomes are related to—but not interchangeable with—diagnostic labels.

## Core diagnostic distinctions
| Entity | Typical architecture | Important caveat |
|---|---|---|
| Follicular adenoma | Follicular | Benign; histologic evaluation is essential |
| Follicular carcinoma | Follicular | Capsular and/or vascular invasion matters |
| Conventional papillary thyroid carcinoma | Often papillary | Characteristic nuclear features are central |
| Follicular variant PTC | Predominantly follicular | Papillary-type nuclei; invasive vs encapsulated distinctions matter |
| NIFTP | Follicular | Requires strict resection-based criteria including absence of invasion; cannot be definitively diagnosed by FNA alone |

## Classification caution
Historical papers describe how papillary architecture was once considered a malignancy indicator even without invasion. That is not a universal current diagnostic rule: WHO recognizes follicular adenoma with papillary architecture and low-risk entities. Check current WHO and Bethesda criteria.
