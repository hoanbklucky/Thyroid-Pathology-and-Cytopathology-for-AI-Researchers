# Thyroid FNA cytology terminology: illustrated descriptions and engineering interpretations

This guide unpacks two example descriptions of **Papanicolaou-stained thyroid FNA cytology** supplied for study: one benign follicular nodule (Bethesda II) and one malignant specimen (Bethesda VI) with **features characteristic of papillary thyroid carcinoma (PTC)**.

**Scope:** These are illustrative descriptions, **not universal diagnostic rules**. Bethesda VI includes other malignancies (such as medullary and anaplastic thyroid carcinoma) with different morphology. A single feature (e.g., a groove or a high nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratio) is not independently diagnostic.

## 1. The two descriptions, annotated

### Example A — Benign follicular nodule, Bethesda II

> “Thyroid FNA cytology, benign nodule, Bethesda II, predominantly scattered macrofollicular groups represented by flat, honeycomb sheets of evenly spaced follicular cells with delicate, wispy, moderate amount of cytoplasm and small dark nuclei. Cell clusters have rounded edges. Nuclear to cytoplasmic ratio is low. Background shows a film of pale blue to green protein that is colloid. Bright field microscope with Papanicolaou stain.”

**In plain language:** The sample contains relatively orderly, flat groups of thyroid follicular cells. The nuclei are small and fairly evenly spaced, with ample cytoplasm around them. The background contains colloid, the protein-rich material normally stored inside thyroid follicles. These findings support a benign follicular nodule when interpreted with the full specimen and clinical context.

### Example B — Malignant cytology, Bethesda VI, PTC-like morphology

> “Thyroid FNA cytology, malignant thyroid FNA, Bethesda VI, crowded follicular clusters with oval, non-round nuclei, longitudinal nuclear grooves, occasional membrane-bound intranuclear pseudo-inclusions, nuclear enlargement with fine, pale, open chromatin. Groups may be numerous and show palisading nuclei at the edges. High nuclear to cytoplasmic ratios. Cytoplasm is dense with sharp edges, nuclei may be slightly angulated. Large cell clusters which are irregularly shaped, with angled edges or papillary in architecture. Background, scattered cells may include macrophages. Bright field microscope with Papanicolaou stain.”

**In plain language:** The cells may form crowded or irregular clusters, with nuclei that are enlarged, oval, overlapping, grooved, or contain cytoplasmic pseudoinclusions. The chromatin can look unusually pale and fine. These are features associated with PTC, although their diagnostic significance depends on their combination, distribution, and the specimen as a whole.

## 2. Cell grouping and architecture

| Term | Pathology meaning | What an image-analysis researcher might look for | Caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Follicular cell** | Epithelial cell lining thyroid follicles; produces thyroid hormones and thyroglobulin | Cell shape, nuclear/cytoplasmic segmentation | Not synonymous with follicular carcinoma |
| **Macrofollicular group** | Cytologic group reflecting relatively large, colloid-rich follicles | Broad sheets or groups with orderly spacing | An FNA smear does not preserve a complete follicle reliably |
| **Honeycomb sheet** | Flat, cohesive monolayer of polygonal cells with regular spacing, resembling a honeycomb | Regular neighbor distances, similar nuclear sizes, little overlap | Thick areas may falsely appear crowded |
| **Flat sheet / monolayer** | Cells lying in approximately one plane | Few overlapping nuclei, visible cell borders | Smear thickness changes appearance |
| **Scattered groups** | Separate groups dispersed across the slide | Group count and spatial distribution | Depends on sampling and smear preparation |
| **Cell cluster** | A connected or overlapping group of cells | Cluster size, contour, cell density | Clusters may fold during slide preparation |
| **Rounded cluster edges** | Smooth, curved perimeter of a cell group | Shape circularity, solidity, edge curvature | Not a stand-alone benign criterion |
| **Crowded follicular clusters** | Follicular epithelial groups with tightly packed/overlapping cells | Nuclear density and overlap | Crowding occurs in multiple benign and malignant conditions |
| **Palisading nuclei** | Nuclei aligned in a row along a cell group's edge | Nuclear orientation and alignment near cluster boundary | Not specific to PTC |
| **Irregular / angled cluster edges** | Angular or jagged outline of a cell group | Boundary curvature, convexity, irregularity | Preparation artifact may alter contours |
| **Papillary architecture / papillary fragment** | Finger-like epithelial projection, classically with a fibrovascular core | Branching cluster with central supporting axis | An FNA fragment cannot establish the entire tumor's histologic growth pattern |
| **Fibrovascular core** | Connective-tissue and blood-vessel stalk supporting a true papilla | Central stromal-looking material inside a papillary fragment | May not be visible or preserved in FNA |

## 3. Cell and nuclear morphology

| Term | Pathology meaning | Visual cue / potential measurement | Caution |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cytoplasm** | Material inside the cell membrane, outside the nucleus | Segmented cytoplasmic area and texture | Boundaries can be faint |
| **Delicate / wispy cytoplasm** | Thin, pale, lightly stained cytoplasm | Low optical density, indistinct borders | Strongly affected by staining and focus |
| **Moderate cytoplasm** | Visible amount of cytoplasm surrounding the nucleus | Cytoplasmic area relative to nuclear area | Subjective unless standardized |
| **Dense cytoplasm** | More intensely stained or compact-appearing cytoplasm | Cytoplasmic optical density and texture | Stain variation can mimic differences |
| **Sharp cytoplasmic edges** | Clearly defined cell boundaries | Edge strength / boundary contrast | Not specific for malignancy |
| **Nucleus** | DNA-containing cell compartment | Nuclear area, perimeter, color, texture | Must separate intact nuclei from debris |
| **Small dark nuclei** | Relatively small, hyperchromatic-appearing nuclei | Nuclear size and optical density | Dark nuclei can reflect preparation or chromatin condensation |
| **Oval / non-round nucleus** | Nucleus elongated along one axis | Aspect ratio, eccentricity | Shape varies naturally and with smearing |
| **Angulated nucleus** | Nuclear outline has corners or sharp bends | Contour curvature / angularity | May be artifact |
| **Nuclear enlargement** | Nuclei larger than expected for comparable follicular cells | Nuclear area distribution, relative size | Calibrate magnification; compare like specimens |
| **Nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratio (N:C ratio)** | Relative size of nucleus compared with cytoplasm | Nuclear area / cytoplasmic area; specify denominator and method | High N:C can result from cell type or poor cytoplasmic preservation |
| **Nuclear crowding / overlap** | Nuclei close together or superimposed | Nearest-neighbor distance, overlap frequency | Smear thickness is a confounder |
| **Nuclear groove** | Linear infolding of nuclear membrane, often appearing as a dark line across nucleus | Elongated intranuclear line / indentation | Can occur in benign/reactive cells; not sufficient alone |
| **Intranuclear cytoplasmic pseudoinclusion** | Cytoplasm invaginated into the nucleus, appearing as a sharply circumscribed inclusion | Round/oval intranuclear area with a defined rim | Different from a groove or optical artifact |
| **Membrane-bound pseudoinclusion** | Pseudoinclusion outlined by nuclear membrane | Clear boundary/rim inside nucleus | Confirmation may require expert review |
| **Chromatin** | DNA-protein material within the nucleus | Nuclear staining distribution and texture | Strongly stain- and preparation-dependent |
| **Fine / pale / open chromatin** | Delicate, dispersed, lightly stained chromatin (sometimes described as optically clear) | Lower contrast, finer nuclear texture | Papanicolaou staining differs from H&E histology |
| **Hyperchromasia** | Darker-than-expected nuclear staining | Nuclear optical density | Not specific for cancer |

### Nuclear-to-cytoplasmic ratio: simple numerical example

If a cell's nuclear area is 30 µm² and its cytoplasmic area **excluding the nucleus** is 90 µm², the **N:C area ratio = 30/90 = 0.33**. Some image-analysis pipelines instead use nuclear area divided by **total cell area** (30/120 = 0.25); document which definition you use, because they are **not interchangeable**.

## 4. Background material, preparation, and microscopy

| Term | Meaning | Typical appearance | Research consideration |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Colloid** | Thyroglobulin-rich proteinaceous material stored in thyroid follicle lumens | Thin watery film or thicker pools; may stain pale blue/green with Pap stain | Amount/appearance depends on sampling and preparation |
| **Proteinaceous background** | Extracellular protein-rich material | Amorphous staining between cells | Not all background protein is colloid |
| **Macrophage** | Immune cell that clears debris; common in cystic/degenerative lesions | Larger cells with abundant, sometimes foamy cytoplasm | Macrophages are **not** a malignancy marker |
| **Bright-field microscopy** | Transmitted visible light through a stained slide | Conventional color microscope image | White balance, exposure, illumination affect pixel values |
| **Papanicolaou (Pap) stain** | Multichromatic cytology stain used to highlight nuclei and cytoplasm | Nuclear and cytoplasmic colors vary with formulation and preparation | Do not assume identical color distributions across laboratories |
| **Fine-needle aspiration (FNA)** | Sampling cells from a nodule using a thin needle | Cells and groups on smear or liquid-based preparation | Does not retain complete tumor capsule or tissue architecture |
| **Bethesda II** | Cytology category: benign | Low estimated category-level ROM | Does not mean absolute zero risk |
| **Bethesda VI** | Cytology category: malignant | High estimated category-level ROM | Does not identify tumor subtype by itself |

## 5. Benign-versus-PTC-like comparison (not a universal classifier)

| Characteristic | Example benign follicular nodule | Example PTC-like malignant cytology |
|---|---|---|
| Cell groups | Flat honeycomb sheets, relatively smooth edges | Crowded, irregular groups; papillary fragments may occur |
| Nuclei | Small, relatively round, evenly spaced | Enlarged, oval/angulated, often crowded |
| Nuclear membrane | Generally smooth | Grooves and/or pseudoinclusions may be present |
| Chromatin | Relatively dark, uniform | May be fine, pale, open |
| N:C ratio | Often lower | Often higher |
| Background | Colloid often prominent | Variable; macrophages are nonspecific |
| Bethesda label in **these examples** | II | VI |

**Caution:** No row is sufficient on its own to classify a nodule. PTC can have subtle nuclei and follicular arrangements; benign/reactive samples can have atypia. MTC and ATC have distinct cytomorphology. Review the [broader visual feature dictionary](VISUAL_FEATURE_DICTIONARY.md).

## 6. Practical exercise for AI researchers

For each educational micrograph, annotate:
1. **Image metadata:** patient/sample ID (deidentified), stain, magnification, preparation, site, quality.
2. **Cell-group morphology:** sheet/cluster/microfollicle/papillary fragment, shape, overlap, border regularity.
3. **Nuclear morphology:** area, aspect ratio, grooves, pseudoinclusions, chromatin appearance, crowding.
4. **Cytoplasm:** estimated area, density, boundary visibility, N:C ratio if segmentation is reliable.
5. **Background:** colloid, macrophages, necrotic debris, blood, artifacts.
6. **Labels and uncertainty:** Bethesda category, surgical diagnosis if available, reviewer confidence, and 'not assessable' flags.

**Avoid shortcut learning:** An image model may learn stain color, magnification, site, or preparation rather than pathology. Validate across institutions and preserve patient-level splits. These feature definitions are intended for pathologist-supervised annotation and model interpretation, not automated standalone diagnosis.
